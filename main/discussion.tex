\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

APT-Bench separates targets with different statistical units and evidentiary
standards. Coarse lineage is easier than fine subtype, most subtype errors cross
lineage, and matched controls show no significant \ourmethod{} advantage. Disease
recognition is strong but sensitive to patient-specific offsets; compact budgets
indicate measurement efficiency, not unique causal biomarkers.

This single-site cohort has only 40 patients, no acquisition-batch metadata, and
no external validation, so biological and technical offsets cannot be separated
and novelty remains exploratory. Reuse comes from the fixed schema, folds, tasks,
and evaluator \citep{luecken2025open,liu2025multitask}, not broad clinical
representativeness; future cohorts should enter as new dataset modules and use
cross-site evaluation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

APT-Bench defines patient-disjoint hierarchy typing, disease audit, and
measurement-efficiency tasks for single-cell APT profiles. External cohorts
remain necessary for clinical claims.
