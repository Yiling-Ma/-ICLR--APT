\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

APT-Bench makes single-cell aptamer profiling a defined cell-typing evaluation
problem: the input is APT alone, the targets are paired-transcriptomic
annotations, and generalization is measured on held-out patients. The
reference suite establishes comparison points across linear, tree-based,
and Transformer recipes. Soft-cascade Transformer supplies an explicit
hierarchy-aware design, but the benchmark does not depend on demonstrating
its superiority or presenting its components as novel algorithms.

\paragraph{Interpreting model and modality comparisons.}
The main table holds the measurement modality fixed; the matched-input probe
instead holds the probe protocol fixed while varying APT and RNA. These
questions must not be conflated. RNA-derived annotations favor interpretation
of RNA performance as internal label reconstruction, not independent truth
validation. Lower fine F1 also reflects different class counts, prevalence,
and boundaries. Full-budget MLP/ResNet coverage and objective-matched cascade
ablations remain incomplete. The retrospective shift to subject-balanced
reporting changes the estimand without changing model checkpoints; historical
cell-weighted scores are preserved for traceability.

\paragraph{From cell predictions to patient use.}
Subtype summaries improve reference-composition recovery over a training-mean
baseline, providing a concrete aggregate-information result even when fine
cell typing remains difficult. This does not imply an incremental benefit
for disease discrimination. Forty participants across six groups provide
limited independent evidence, and patient-disjoint folds cannot remove
disease-correlated acquisition effects. Centering can remove biological as
well as technical variation. A compact panel that preserves this mixed signal
is consequently not a validated diagnostic panel, and inference-cell
subsampling does not establish reduced training requirements.

\paragraph{Scope and next validation.}
The benchmark is conditional on one APT cohort and its operational taxonomy.
Independent annotation review, completed dataset provenance and access
documentation, and portable reproduction remain necessary release work.
A subsequent clinical study must freeze the assay and model, document input
volume and cell recovery, record acquisition batches, and test an independent
APT cohort. External RNA datasets broaden the sampling audit, not the
clinical evidence for this assay. These boundaries retain blood-based
characterization as the motivation without treating one-drop diagnosis as
an established result.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

APT-Bench establishes patient-disjoint evaluation of lineage and subtype
prediction from single-cell aptamer profiles, with classical and neural
references including Soft-cascade Transformer. Separate APT-only and
paired-input comparisons distinguish architecture performance from modality
effects. Nested patient aggregation demonstrates reference-composition
recovery while leaving incremental disease utility unresolved. The benchmark
provides a basis for improving APT cell models and evaluating their downstream
use; acquisition-aware independent validation is required before clinical
claims.
