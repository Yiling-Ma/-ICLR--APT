\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

APT-Bench is primarily an evaluation contribution rather than a claim of model
superiority. Across matched neural and classical models, broad lineage is more
reliably recovered than transcriptome-derived subtype, and no
\ourmethod{}-specific advantage is statistically supported. The resulting
granularity gap and the degradation from gold to OOF-predicted subtype
composition show that additional model complexity alone does not resolve fine
cell typing or prevent its errors from propagating to patient-level summaries.

The benchmark also makes the statistical unit explicit. Although it contains
361{,}792 cells, clinical generalization is supported by only 40 independent
patients. A cell-level split therefore does more than inflate absolute scores:
in our protocol-sensitivity analysis, it substantially changes the apparent
relative gains between models. Additional cells can stabilize within-patient
aggregation, but they cannot substitute for additional patients. Clinical
single-cell benchmarks should consequently report both patient-independent
generalization and measurement budgets rather than treating the split or assay
size as secondary implementation details.

The disease analyses delimit what can be concluded from this cohort. Strong
centering sensitivity indicates that patient-specific absolute-expression
structure contributes substantially to within-cohort discrimination, while the
compact-panel results establish measurement efficiency and redundancy rather
than causal biomarkers. The cohort is single-site, lacks acquisition-batch
metadata, and has no external validation; the LODO analysis is descriptive and
cross-disease generalization remains unresolved. The fixed schema, patient
splits, tasks, and evaluator are reusable in future APT and clinical single-cell
cohorts \citep{luecken2025open,liu2025multitask}, but batch-aware, cross-site
validation is required before clinical claims.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

APT-Bench establishes a patient-disjoint evaluation contract for hierarchical
cell typing, disease audit, and measurement efficiency from single-cell aptamer
profiles. Across matched neural and classical models, coarse lineage is
consistently easier than fine transcriptome-derived subtype prediction, and no
\ourmethod{}-specific advantage is statistically supported. Within-cohort
disease discrimination saturates under compact aptamer and inference-cell
budgets, but is strongly associated with patient-specific absolute-expression
structure. These findings position APT-Bench as a benchmark for evaluating
statistical-unit, granularity, and measurement effects, while external cohorts
remain necessary for clinical claims.
