\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The motivating question is whether blood-derived single-cell APT profiles
can support both immune-cell identification and characterization of the
patient's disease state. Our results show that these are connected but
non-equivalent evaluation targets. APT measurements support lineage and subtype
prediction at different performance levels, while patient summaries can retain
disease-associated variation that is not reducible to validated cell abundance.
A joint evaluation is useful precisely because success at one level does not
establish success at the other.

\paragraph{What is learned about cell identity?}
The paired-input diagnostic places APT performance in the context of the
transcriptomic labels it is asked to reconstruct. Stronger RNA reconstruction
under the matched probe suggests input-dependent accessibility of these labels,
not a proven information-theoretic limit of aptamers. RNA-informed annotation,
fixed HVG selection, class imbalance, and uncertain subtype boundaries remain
alternative contributors. Independent annotation review and controlled
within-lineage tests are needed before assigning the residual difficulty to
biological resolution alone. The Transformer comparisons evaluate reference
recipes, not an established algorithmic advance.

\paragraph{What is learned about patient state?}
The nested bridge demonstrates disease-associated information in cell-model
outputs, but does not show that improved subtype typing improves disease
characterization. Direct APT prediction and composition-based prediction can
use different information. Patient-centering sensitivity and strong identity
recognition make absolute-expression structure an important explanation to
test, rather than a validated biomarker. Forty patients across six groups
provide limited independent evidence, regardless of how many cells are
measured. COMBAT and OneK1K broaden the sampling audit but do not resolve these
APT-specific clinical questions.

\paragraph{From research assay to blood-based application.}
A credible next study would freeze the assay, preprocessing, annotation,
and prediction pipeline, document input volume and cell recovery, record
acquisition batches, and evaluate an independent APT cohort. Biological and
technical variation must be distinguished through study design, not only
post-hoc adjustment. The present compact-panel and inference-cell results
describe redundancy within this cohort; they cannot establish a minimal blood
volume, a validated diagnostic panel, or equivalent training on fewer cells.

The supporting scaling analysis adds a practical caution: natural allocation
and fixed-label allocation estimate different benefits of recruiting subjects.
The latter also changes within-class donor allocation in the current
implementation. Joint stability ranges are not confidence intervals for the
seed-averaged effect. These findings qualify resource-allocation conclusions
without replacing the central cell-to-patient question.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

APT-Bench evaluates a common blood-derived molecular input at two levels:
immune-cell identity and patient disease-associated state. Patient-disjoint
cell typing, paired-input reconstruction, and nested cell-to-patient transfer
separate what the profiles predict from what those predictions establish.
APT supports measurable information at both levels, but fine-subtype utility
and the biological origin of disease-associated offsets remain unresolved.
The benchmark provides a structured basis for improving and validating
blood-based characterization, not a claim that reliable one-drop disease
diagnosis has already been achieved.
