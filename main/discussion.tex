\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The central lesson of APT-Bench is that the unit of generalization must be
part of the cell-typing benchmark. The observed cell-split optimism is
consistent across the two evaluated model families and both resolutions,
but its magnitude is specific to the available protocol comparisons.
Patient-disjoint testing and subject-balanced scoring address different
issues: the former separates people across training and test sets, while
the latter limits unequal cell recovery from dominating the score.

\paragraph{Benchmark contribution versus model contribution.}
The resource specifies an APT input, operational ontology, fixed patient folds,
metrics, and reference results. Soft-cascade is a hierarchy-aware reference,
not a claimed algorithmic advance. The value of the benchmark must therefore
be judged by the relevance of its task, evaluation validity, and usability,
not by whether that reference wins. Complete data access, permissions,
annotation provenance, and a portable evaluator remain necessary release
requirements; incomplete delivery cannot be repaired by stronger novelty
language. Full-budget MLP coverage and more comprehensive APT-compatible
annotation baselines also remain limitations.

\paragraph{Boundaries of the evidence.}
The APT cohort contains 40 participants and RNA-derived reference labels.
Independent annotation checks and additional APT cohorts are needed to
assess broader generalization. Patient-disjoint testing does not remove
disease-correlated acquisition effects, so the secondary disease study is
not clinical validation. Paired RNA contextualizes label reconstruction
rather than establishing an aptamer information limit. Subject--cell sampling
analyses are preserved in a separate report; neither a scaling law nor a
resource-allocation algorithm is a contribution of this paper.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

APT-Bench specifies patient-disjoint cell typing from single-cell aptamer
profiles and establishes classical and neural reference results under an
explicit patient-aware metric. The evaluated cell-level split controls give
consistently higher performance than the new-patient protocol, demonstrating
why held-out cells must not substitute for held-out patients in reporting
generalization. The benchmark's next requirements are complete reproducible
release and independent APT validation, not an unsupported claim of algorithmic
or clinical superiority.
