\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

APT-Bench is an evaluation contribution rather than a claim of model
superiority. Broad lineage is more reliably recovered than
transcriptome-derived subtype, and no soft-cascade advantage is statistically
supported. The paired-RNA probe shows that limited APT resolution contributes
to this gap, while the residual RNA gap and resolved APT+RNA improvement rule
out a single explanation. Annotation uncertainty, modality resolution, and
patient shift remain inseparable without independent reference labels.

The scaling audit illustrates why many cells cannot be equated with many
independent subjects. Patient-disjoint evaluation prevents direct identity
leakage, but an unmatched subject/cell reallocation still changes the training
label distribution. Once class support and quotas are fixed, effects are small,
model- and cohort-dependent, and unresolved under joint training-subset and
test-subject uncertainty. External frontiers plateau over the tested range, but
do not identify a universal allocation rule. Clinical single-cell benchmarks
should therefore report both subject and cell budgets, make class-distribution
changes explicit, and propagate uncertainty from both sampled units.

Patient characterization has a separate identifiability boundary. Nested cell
outputs retain disease-associated variation, but fine composition has no
resolved increment over lineage or recorded nuisance summaries. Strong
centering sensitivity and missing acquisition metadata prevent separation of
biological disease signal from patient-specific or technical structure. COMBAT
and OneK1K validate portability of the scaling protocol, not cancer recognition;
batch-aware external APT cohorts are required for clinical claims.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

APT-Bench establishes a patient-disjoint contract for hierarchical typing,
two-axis scaling, and confounding-aware patient characterization. It reveals a
robust coarse--fine granularity gap and shows that naive fixed-total scaling can
mistake label-distribution changes for subject-breadth benefits. After class
matching, no LR or XGBoost effect is resolved across APT, COMBAT, and OneK1K,
and nested transfer establishes association rather than subtype mediation.
These results position statistical unit, label distribution, and measurement
budget as explicit benchmark dimensions while leaving clinical validity to
batch-aware external evaluation.
