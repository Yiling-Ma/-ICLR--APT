\section{Introduction}

Single-cell aptamer profiling creates a targeted molecular representation of
blood-derived immune cells. To assess whether this representation supports
cell typing, a model must be evaluated on patients absent from training, not
merely on additional cells from familiar patients. The distinction matters
because a large cell count does not create an equally large set of independent
biological samples.

APT-Bench addresses this evaluation problem with 361{,}792 cells from 40
participants, 293 aptamer measurements per cell, and paired-transcriptomic
annotations defining five lineages and 27 subtypes. Its central contribution
is a patient-disjoint cell-typing benchmark specification and empirical
evaluation for the APT modality. We fix the outer patient folds, state the
label ontology and input contract, and report subject-balanced pooled
Macro-F1 so that highly sampled patients do not dominate the score.

The central empirical finding concerns the evaluation protocol. In the
available XGBoost and Soft-cascade comparisons, cell-level splitting gives
higher coarse and fine Macro-F1 than patient-disjoint evaluation in all four
model--resolution comparisons. The increases range from 0.050 to 0.108 on
the historical cell-weighted metric. Thus evaluation on held-out cells from
known patients can give a substantially more favorable picture than evaluation
on new patients. These protocol comparisons are not a matched causal estimate
of leakage alone; they nevertheless show why the intended generalization unit
must be fixed before interpreting performance.

Classical and Transformer models provide reference performance under that
contract. Soft-cascade Transformer is one hierarchy-aware reference, not a
separate algorithmic contribution or the basis for a superiority claim.
We retain an internal paired-RNA reconstruction diagnostic and a short
patient-level application study to contextualize the benchmark, rather than
treating them as additional headline contributions. Subject--cell sampling
experiments are preserved in a separate supporting report.

The paper makes one contribution with three concrete components:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A specified evaluation contract:} APT-only lineage and subtype
    prediction with a fixed ontology, patient-disjoint folds, and explicit
    cell-weighted versus subject-balanced metrics.
    \item \textbf{A split-protocol audit:} cell-level split controls yield higher
    scores in both evaluated model families at both label resolutions,
    demonstrating why held-out-cell and new-patient performance must be
    distinguished.
    \item \textbf{Reference results and an auditable interface:} classical and
    neural predictions, metric code, split manifests, and documented artifact
    checks establish comparison points for subsequent APT models.
\end{itemize}

A complete public benchmark also requires accessible data, verified permissions,
and portable reproduction. Those release requirements remain explicit where
unfinished; a public source repository alone does not establish their completion.
The study does not claim validated blood-based diagnosis, a universal sampling
law, or priority over all prior aptamer evaluation resources.
