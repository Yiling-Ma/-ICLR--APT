\section{Introduction}

A blood sample contains information about its constituent immune cells and
variation associated with the person from whom it was collected. A single-cell
assay can therefore be asked two connected questions: which cells are present,
and what do their measurements reveal about the patient's disease state?
Single-cell aptamer profiling offers a setting in which to evaluate both
questions using the same molecular measurements. The long-term motivation is
minimally invasive blood-based characterization; reliable diagnosis from a
specified small blood volume is an eventual validation target, not an outcome
established by this study.

The primary aim of this paper is a benchmark for APT-only cell typing in new
patients, with a documented hierarchy-aware reference model. Patient-level
disease characterization is a secondary application, not an equally validated
clinical endpoint. Moving from the motivation to an evaluation requires separating cell identity
from patient state. A model can distinguish broad immune lineages without
reconstructing transcriptome-defined subtypes. Conversely, a disease classifier
can exploit patient-level measurement offsets without learning cell composition.
High performance at one level therefore need not validate the other. Correlated
cells also cannot substitute for independent patients when estimating disease
generalization. These distinctions motivate a benchmark that connects the two
levels while testing the limits of their interpretation.

We introduce APT-Bench, comprising 361{,}792 labeled single-cell profiles from
40 participants in five cancer groups and healthy controls. Each cell has 293
aptamer measurements and paired-transcriptome-derived labels in a five-lineage,
27-subtype ontology. The benchmark evaluates APT-only cell typing and
within-cohort patient characterization under fixed patient-disjoint folds.
A Soft-cascade Transformer combines global subtype prediction with softly
routed lineage experts as one reference alongside linear, tree-based, and
other Transformer models. Benchmark validity does not depend on that model
winning, and the current comparisons do not isolate its component effects.
A separate paired-RNA diagnostic asks how reconstructability depends on the input
modality, while strictly nested cross-fitting connects predicted cell profiles
to patient-level disease labels without exposing test patients during either
learning stage (Figure~\ref{fig:cell_patient_overview}).

The results establish reference performance for a targeted molecular input
rather than a diagnostic success claim. Fine typing remains difficult under
the evaluated models. A matched-input probe reconstructs the RNA-derived
labels more strongly from RNA; that modality contrast is not evidence of
algorithm superiority on identical inputs. In the secondary application,
predicted subtype summaries improve reference-composition recovery over a
training-mean baseline. Disease association remains a distinct question, with
unresolved incremental subtype value and acquisition-related explanations.

Reliability is therefore part of the biological question, not an independent
promise of clinical validity. Patient-disjoint splits, subject-balanced metrics,
and acquisition-sensitivity controls delimit what can be concluded from this
cohort. Supporting sampling experiments on APT, COMBAT, and OneK1K further show
why subject count, cell count, and training label distribution must be
distinguished. These external datasets evaluate sampling behavior; they do not
independently validate APT disease prediction.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{An APT cell-typing benchmark.} We specify a paired-annotation dataset, fixed patient partitions, ontology, and patient-aware evaluation for a 293-channel single-cell aptamer input.
    \item \textbf{A reference-model suite.} Classical and Transformer recipes, including a documented Soft-cascade reference, establish APT-only comparison points; a separate paired-input diagnostic assesses modality dependence.
    \item \textbf{A cell-to-patient application study.} Nested aggregation tests reference-composition recovery and exploratory disease association, with explicit controls for alternative sources of predictive signal.
\end{itemize}
