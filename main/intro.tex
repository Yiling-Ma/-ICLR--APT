\section{Introduction}

Single-cell aptamer profiling measures targeted binding patterns alongside
transcriptomic annotations. Apt-seq established joint aptamer and transcriptome
sequencing \citep{delley2018combined}; the measurement principle is not new.
We ask two empirical questions: which levels of cell identity can APT recover
in patients absent from training, and does paired APT add typing information
for cells that an RNA-only reference struggles to distinguish?
Neither question presupposes a positive answer. General single-cell benchmarks provide reusable
task definitions \citep{luecken2025open}, but gene-expression inputs do not
directly define an evaluation contract for a fixed aptamer panel. A
modality-specific reference task must distinguish additional cells from
additional independent patients.

Clinical single-cell collections can contain hundreds of thousands of cells
from only tens of people. APT profiles may encode both cell identity and
patient-associated shifts; cell-random evaluation can reward the latter
without establishing cross-patient typing. Low fine-subtype scores alone
cannot distinguish limited APT information from annotation ambiguity,
imbalance, preprocessing, or model limitations. A useful benchmark therefore
needs explicit statistical units, reference models, and error diagnostics,
rather than interpreting a leaderboard as an assay information limit.

APT-Bench specifies this task with 361{,}792 cells from 40 participants,
293 APT measurements, and RNA-derived labels for five lineages and 27
subtypes. Five fixed patient-disjoint folds and subject-balanced pooled
Macro-F1 define the primary comparison. Classical and neural recipes establish
reference performance; Soft-cascade is one hierarchy-aware reference, not a
separate algorithmic contribution. A true-lineage decoding diagnostic, compared
with training-only conditional priors, separates the benefit of narrowing the
candidate set from evidence of within-lineage discrimination. A matched-budget
RNA probe tests incremental information, with patient- and lineage-conditioned
pairing controls. Patient-disjoint evaluation underpins both questions; it is
not presented as a new statistical principle. Sampling analyses remain in a
separate report, and composition recovery is a short application case study.

The study combines one benchmark with two empirical investigations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A specified evaluation contract:} APT-only lineage and subtype
    prediction with a fixed ontology, patient-disjoint folds, and explicit
    cell-weighted versus subject-balanced metrics.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical recoverability:} ordinary and true-lineage
    decoding, training-prior controls, and results for every lineage probe
    where reference-model errors occur, without treating their scores as an
    intrinsic information limit of APT.
    \item \textbf{Incremental paired information:} matched RNA/APT probes
    and targeted negative controls distinguish a possible cell-pairing
    contribution from patient structure, lineage correspondence, and added
    input dimensions. Repeated conditional controls remain necessary before
    drawing the corresponding substantive conclusion.
\end{itemize}

A complete public benchmark also requires accessible data, verified permissions,
and portable reproduction. Those release requirements remain explicit where
unfinished; a public source repository alone does not establish their completion.
The study does not claim validated blood-based diagnosis, a universal sampling
law, or priority over all prior aptamer evaluation resources.
RNA-derived annotations favor reconstruction from their source modality;
any APT increment is relative to the tested RNA representation and model,
not evidence that RNA intrinsically lacks the corresponding information.
