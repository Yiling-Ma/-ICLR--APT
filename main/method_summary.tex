\section{Benchmark Reference Models}
\label{sec:method_summary}

\paragraph{APT-only comparisons.}
LR and XGBoost provide linear and nonlinear tabular references. Flat and HCE
Transformers and a Soft-cascade Transformer provide backbone-matched neural
recipes. All use APT at inference and the same outer patient partitions.
Training and validation follow each recipe's documented protocol; identical
outer folds do not imply identical training-set sizes or fully matched tuning.
Table~\ref{tab:main_training_protocols} documents all six main-table recipes,
including selection-stage versus final-fit data, weighting, seeds and
recoverable training budgets.
Linear SVM, Random Forest, and reference correlation are additional
appendix references. A plain MLP uses all development cells in each outer fold,
with three fixed training seeds, inner-patient selection, and outer refitting.
Its 256/128-unit ReLU network uses dropout 0.1, AdamW, and training-fold
standardization; the inner fold selects weight decay and epoch (at most 50).
This completes a full-cell-budget reference, not an objective- or
optimizer-matched ablation of the Transformers.

\paragraph{Hierarchy-aware reference.}
Soft-cascade Transformer embeds aptamer identities and values and combines
a global subtype head with lineage-specific experts weighted by coarse
probabilities. Appendix~\ref{sec:method} specifies the routing equation and
training objective.
The independent coarse and fine heads are evaluated without constrained
decoding. Flat removes the cascade; HCE instead uses a two-level hierarchical
loss adaptation \citep{cultrera2026improving}. Only Soft-cascade uses
cross-disease supervised contrastive pairing, so their objectives and
auxiliary disease-label usage differ. These are reference-recipe comparisons,
not component ablations. No routing or contrastive contribution is claimed
in isolation. Appendix~\ref{sec:method} gives the full architecture, losses,
hyperparameters, and component matrix.

\paragraph{Input compatibility.}
Gene-based pretrained models such as scGPT and Geneformer
\citep{cui2024scgpt,theodoris2023transfer} do not directly accept anonymous
aptamer feature IDs without a verified mapping or a separately trained adapter.
Supplying paired RNA would change the input contract. This distinguishes an
undefined direct APT input from an adaptable but unevaluated model; it does
not justify claiming superiority over transcriptomic annotation methods.

\paragraph{Matched-input diagnostics.}
A separate matched-input probe varies APT and RNA while holding its training
budget and patient folds fixed. Training-only subtype priors and true-lineage
masking provide candidate-set controls. APT shuffling within patient, or within
patient and true lineage, is a privileged diagnostic rather than a deployable
model input (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:identity_questions}).
RNA also supplies the reference annotations, so this is an internal
reconstruction comparison, not independent label validation. HVG selection
and scaling are fitted separately within each inner and outer training
partition. Table~\ref{tab:identity_training_config} gives the input transforms,
training weights, optimization settings and selection grids.
