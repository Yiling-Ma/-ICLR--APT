\section{Hierarchy-Aware APT Models and Patient Readouts}
\label{sec:method_summary}

\paragraph{Hierarchy-aware cell-identity references.}
The first APT-Bench contract is APT-only prediction of immune-cell identity in
held-out patients. We therefore include a hierarchy-aware neural reference as a
main method, not only as an appendix diagnostic. Each cell is represented by a
293-dimensional standardized APT vector. The Transformer backbone embeds
aptamer identity and value as tokens, prepends a learnable \texttt{[CLS]} token,
and uses the final \texttt{[CLS]} state $z$ as the cell representation.

Let $M=5$ lineages and $K=\numsubtypes{}$ subtypes define the fixed ontology,
with parent map $m(k)$. The soft-cascade reference predicts lineage logits
$c=W_cz$ and routing probabilities $p(m\mid x)=\operatorname{softmax}(c)_m$.
Subtype logits combine a global subtype path and lineage-specific experts:
\begin{equation}
s_k(x)=g_k(z)+\alpha\,p(m(k)\mid x)e_{m(k),k}(z)
+\gamma\log\{\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)p(m(k)\mid x)\}.
\label{eq:main_soft_cascade}
\end{equation}
We use $\alpha=1$, $\epsilon=0.1$, and ramp $\gamma$ from 0 to 0.5 during early
training. The loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fine}}
+0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coarse}}
+w(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{contrast}},
\label{eq:main_cascade_loss}
\end{equation}
where $w(t)$ ramps to 0.2. The contrastive term uses same-subtype,
cross-disease cells as positives and different-subtype cells as negatives,
with additional emphasis on within-lineage negatives. This construction is
intended to provide a reproducible hierarchy-aware reference for the benchmark;
disease recognition itself is evaluated separately at the patient level.

The neural ladder compares the soft cascade with a flat Transformer and the
same Transformer trained with a two-level HCE adaptation
\citep{cultrera2026improving}. These references share the APT tokenizer,
encoder dimensions, optimizer, training budget, patient folds, training-only
preprocessing, and validation fine-macro-F1 checkpoint rule. Their objectives
are not fully matched: only the soft-cascade model uses disease labels for
cross-disease contrastive pairing, whereas Flat and HCE use classification
losses alone. Thus the ladder is a benchmark reference comparison, not a
component ablation; no routing, HCE, or contrastive effect is claimed in
isolation. Appendix~\ref{sec:method} records implementation details and the
component matrix.

Baseline coverage is intentionally distinguished from input compatibility.
LR and XGBoost are included in the APT hierarchy summary, which
also reports Linear SVM, Random Forest, and reference correlation alongside
the three Transformer recipes. These are not an exhaustive model leaderboard.
Plain MLP/ResNet and additional modern tabular learners are applicable to the
APT matrix but lack completed full hierarchy evaluations in this release;
we do not classify their absence as incompatibility or underperformance.
HCE supplies a hierarchy-loss reference, not comprehensive coverage of
single-cell annotation systems.

Pretrained transcriptomic encoders such as scGPT and Geneformer use gene-based
inputs \citep{cui2024scgpt,theodoris2023transfer}. The anonymous APT feature IDs
have no verified public gene-token mapping, so direct use of those checkpoints
is not defined for the APT-only task. Learning an APT adapter would introduce
an additional training protocol, while supplying paired RNA would change the
input modality. On the external RNA cohorts, transcriptomic encoders are
applicable but unevaluated here, rather than intrinsically incompatible.
We make no claim that computational expense alone excludes them, nor that
unrun models underperform the reported references.

\paragraph{Nested cell-to-patient bridge.}
For the composition bridge, each disease outer fold seals eight patients. A
final disease-label-free XGBoost subtype model trained on the other 32 patients
constructs outer-test compositions. Downstream training compositions for those
32 patients are instead produced by four inner patient-disjoint fits, each
trained on 24 patients. A class-weighted multinomial LR, with regularization
selected by patient-level inner CV, is then evaluated once on the sealed eight
patients. Thus neither the cell-typing stage nor the disease stage uses an
outer-test patient during preprocessing, feature construction, fitting, or
selection. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:composition_bridge} provides the complete
data flow and controls. A conditional increment audit uses the same nested
features and tuning protocol to compare recorded QC plus predicted lineage
against recorded QC plus predicted subtype composition; subtype probabilities
implicitly contain their lineage sums.

\paragraph{Direct measurement summaries and controls.}
Patient-median APT profiles provide a route to disease-group prediction that
does not require inferred cell identities. Cell-level disease-source predictions
with patient majority voting form a separate diagnostic. We compare these
associations with patient-wise centering, patient-identity recognition, and
recorded QC summaries rather than treating high within-cohort scores as
clinical validation. Full settings are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:disease_patient_level}.
