\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Clinical and single-cell evaluation.}
Multi-cancer detection commonly uses circulating DNA, methylation,
fragmentomics, or bulk proteins \citep{cohen2018detection,zeng2026pan,wan2025promises,kahwati2025multicancer}.
APT profiling instead observes many correlated cells from each blood draw, so
patient characterization and cell typing require different statistical units.
Public single-cell benchmarks increasingly separate datasets, representations,
and evaluators and use study- or patient-separated partitions
\citep{luecken2025open,liu2025multitask,shitov2025spare,liu2026pascient}; none
combines a fixed aptamer panel, coarse-to-fine typing, two-axis training scale,
and a confounding-aware patient audit.

\paragraph{Representation and hierarchy.}
Transcriptome foundation models such as Geneformer, scGPT, and scFoundation are
not directly matched to a fixed 293-dimensional protein-oriented panel, and
recent evaluations show that simple baselines can remain competitive when the
protocol is controlled \citep{theodoris2023transfer,cui2024scgpt,hao2024large,kedzierska2025zero}.
Cell identity is hierarchical, motivating structured losses and models
\citep{jia2025schdeepinsight,cultrera2026improving}. We therefore compare
XGBoost with flat, HCE, and soft-cascade Transformer recipes. The neural models
match backbone and budget but not complete objectives, so they do not constitute
a component ablation.

\paragraph{Measurement budgets and tabular baselines.}
Clinical high-plex assays must distinguish feature-measurement cost from the
number of cells used at training or inference. We treat compact-panel and
inference-cell analyses only as redundancy sensitivities of a mixed
disease-associated signal. Gradient-boosted trees are strong medium-dimensional
tabular baselines \citep{grinsztajn2022tree,erickson2026tabarena}, and remain
competitive here; the benchmark consequently emphasizes evaluation rather than
architectural superiority.
