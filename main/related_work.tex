\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Liquid Biopsy and Multi-Cancer Early Detection} 
Blood-based multi-cancer early detection (MCED) gained broad attention with CancerSEEK, which combined circulating protein biomarkers with cell-free DNA mutation profiles to detect and localize eight cancer types from a single blood draw \citep{cohen2018detection}. Subsequent MCED studies have largely focused on cell-free molecular signals, including circulating tumor DNA, DNA methylation, fragmentomic features, and circulating protein biomarkers \citep{zeng2026pan}. Recent clinical reviews highlight the substantial potential of these approaches while emphasizing unresolved challenges for population-level deployment, including calibration across cancer types, false-positive burden, and the lack of completed randomized trials demonstrating a mortality benefit \citep{wan2025promises, kahwati2025multicancer}. Recent studies have shown that peripheral blood immune profiles capture systematic cancer-associated variation and clinically informative immune states \citep{dyikanov2024comprehensive, he2026multimodal}. This cellular resolution changes the prediction objective. Beyond determining whether a blood-derived signal indicates cancer, we ask whether the same assay can characterize the associated immune response through hierarchical lineage and subtype predictions. Our work therefore extends the conventional disease-level MCED formulation toward an integrated diagnostic and immunological readout from a single blood sample.
\paragraph{Single-Cell Foundation Models}
Large pretrained models for single-cell transcriptomics, including Geneformer \citep{theodoris2023transfer}, scGPT \citep{cui2024scgpt}, and scFoundation \citep{hao2024large}, learn general-purpose cell representations from large collections of scRNA-seq profiles and support downstream tasks such as cell-type annotation. These models are not directly applicable to our setting for two reasons. First, their input representations are designed for transcriptomic measurements, typically using rank-based or bin-based encodings over gene vocabularies. In contrast, our data consist of a 293-dimensional aptamer-based protein panel and therefore do not directly conform to the gene-token input spaces on which these models were pretrained. Second, recent systematic zero-shot evaluations show that Geneformer and scGPT, without task-specific fine-tuning, can be matched or outperformed by substantially simpler baselines, including highly variable gene features and untrained random embeddings, on downstream classification and clustering tasks \citep{kedzierska2025zero}. These results suggest that representations learned from large-scale transcriptomic pretraining do not necessarily transfer reliably even to downstream tasks within the RNA modality. Given both the modality mismatch and the uncertain benefit of direct transfer, we train \ourmethod{} directly on APT protein expression rather than adapting an RNA-pretrained foundation model to an input modality absent from its pretraining data.
\paragraph{Hierarchical Classification and Novelty Detection} Cell-type identity naturally follows a coarse-to-fine ontology, motivating classification methods that explicitly incorporate hierarchical structure. Recent work has shown that aligning learning objectives with cell-type hierarchies can improve both classification accuracy and generalization. scHDeepInsight introduces an adaptive hierarchical focal loss and achieves strong performance across fine-grained immune cell subtypes \citep{jia2025schdeepinsight}. Similarly, hierarchical cross-entropy objectives that allow predictions to defer to a parent category when fine-grained evidence is insufficient improve out-of-distribution performance across model architectures \citep{cultrera2026improving}. \ourmethod{} follows the same general principle but incorporates hierarchy directly into the prediction architecture rather than only through the training objective. Specifically, fine-level predictions are produced through a continuously weighted mixture of lineage-conditioned experts while retaining an unconditional global prediction path, as described in \S\ref{sec:method:cascade}. This design allows uncertainty in the coarse prediction to reduce, rather than completely determine, the contribution of lineage-specific experts. A complementary challenge is identifying inputs that do not belong to the training distribution. We consider two established training-free approaches as baselines for novelty detection, maximum softmax probability \citep{hendrycks2016baseline} and energy-based scoring \citep{liu2020energy}. Recent evaluation in single-cell transcriptomics has shown that energy-based scoring provides a strong training-free baseline for detecting novel cell types under realistic biological distribution shifts \citep{theunissen2025evaluation}. We therefore compare both approaches with a dedicated novelty head trained on the shared hierarchical representation of \ourmethod{} in \S\ref{sec:method:novelty}. Unlike confidence scores derived only from classification logits, the trained head directly learns a novelty decision from the representation produced by the cascade, enabling explicit evaluation of whether cells from an entirely unseen disease can be distinguished from cells associated with diseases observed during training.

\paragraph{Tabular Deep Learning versus Classical Machine Learning}
APT expression is naturally represented as tabular data, with each cell encoded as a fixed-length, non-sequential feature vector. Prior work has shown that gradient-boosted tree ensembles remain highly competitive with, and often outperform, deep neural networks on medium-scale tabular datasets, reflecting distinct inductive biases between the two model families \citep{grinsztajn2022tree}. More recent large-scale benchmarking supports this observation, identifying gradient-boosted trees as strong and practical defaults for tabular prediction, while deep tabular models often require substantially greater tuning and ensembling budgets to achieve comparable performance \citep{erickson2026tabarena}. These findings provide important context for our results. This context is directly relevant to our own findings: under patient-level bootstrap evaluation, \ourmethod{} matches but does not significantly exceed XGBoost on fine-subtype macro-F1 (Table~\ref{tab:main}), consistent with this broader literature. We therefore ground \ourmethod{}'s advantage in the hierarchical consistency and per-cell novelty detection discussed in \S\ref{sec:results} and \S\ref{sec:discussion}, rather than in a raw performance margin over XGBoost.
