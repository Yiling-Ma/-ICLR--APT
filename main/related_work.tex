\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Evaluation across cells and subjects.}
Reusable single-cell benchmarks increasingly separate datasets, models, and
metrics and adopt sample- or study-disjoint evaluation
\citep{luecken2025open,liu2025multitask,shitov2025spare,liu2026pascient}.
These practices prevent direct leakage, but do not by themselves isolate the
effect of subject breadth when a fixed-total subsample changes the training
label distribution. Our contribution complements patient-disjoint splitting
with class-matched allocation and joint development/test uncertainty.

\paragraph{Single-cell representations and hierarchy.}
Geneformer, scGPT, and scFoundation motivate large transcriptomic
representations, while controlled studies show that simple baselines can remain
competitive \citep{theodoris2023transfer,cui2024scgpt,hao2024large,kedzierska2025zero}.
Cell annotation also motivates hierarchical losses and architectures
\citep{jia2025schdeepinsight,cultrera2026improving}. We use flat, HCE, and
soft-cascade Transformers plus XGBoost as an APT case study. Because their
complete objectives are not matched, this ladder illustrates the evaluation
setting rather than constituting the primary method or a component ablation.

\paragraph{Clinical assays and measurement budgets.}
Multi-cancer detection commonly uses circulating DNA, methylation,
fragmentomics, or bulk proteins
\citep{cohen2018detection,zeng2026pan,wan2025promises,kahwati2025multicancer}.
Single-cell APT instead observes many correlated cells from each blood draw,
making subject, training-cell, feature, and inference-cell budgets distinct.
Gradient-boosted trees are strong tabular baselines
\citep{grinsztajn2022tree,erickson2026tabarena}; compact-panel analyses are kept
secondary because feature redundancy cannot resolve biological versus technical
confounding.
