\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Blood-based characterization.}
Multi-cancer detection commonly uses circulating DNA, methylation,
fragmentomics, or bulk proteins
\citep{cohen2018detection,zeng2026pan,wan2025promises,kahwati2025multicancer}.
Our question is complementary: whether single-cell aptamer measurements can
support both immune-cell identification and patient disease-associated
characterization. APT-Bench evaluates that connection within a research cohort;
it does not equate within-cohort classification with prospective screening.

\paragraph{Single-cell representations and hierarchy.}
Geneformer, scGPT, and scFoundation motivate large transcriptomic
representations, while controlled studies show that simple baselines can remain
competitive \citep{theodoris2023transfer,cui2024scgpt,hao2024large,kedzierska2025zero}.
Cell annotation also motivates hierarchical losses and architectures
\citep{jia2025schdeepinsight,cultrera2026improving}. We use flat, HCE, and
soft-cascade Transformers alongside classical models as APT references. Because their
complete objectives are not matched, this ladder illustrates the evaluation
setting rather than constituting a new learning algorithm or component ablation.

\paragraph{Evaluation across cells and subjects.}
Reusable single-cell benchmarks separate datasets, models, and metrics and
adopt sample- or study-disjoint evaluation
\citep{luecken2025open,liu2025multitask,shitov2025spare,liu2026pascient}.
APT-Bench links cell and patient tasks with nested cross-fitting and makes
their statistical units explicit. Gradient-boosted trees provide strong
tabular references \citep{grinsztajn2022tree,erickson2026tabarena}.
Supporting sampling and feature-budget audits address correlated observations
and resource choices, but cannot resolve biological versus technical confounding.
