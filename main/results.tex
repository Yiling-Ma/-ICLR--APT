\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Cell-Level Splitting Gives an Optimistic Evaluation}
\label{sec:results:protocol}

The benchmark first asks whether evaluation on additional cells represents
generalization to additional patients. Table~\ref{tab:protocol_sensitivity}
contrasts the historical cell-level split controls with the frozen
patient-disjoint evaluation. Cell-level splitting gives higher scores for
both models at both resolutions: $+0.050/+0.069$ coarse/fine Macro-F1 for
XGBoost and $+0.090/+0.108$ for Soft-cascade. The apparent fine advantage of
Soft-cascade over XGBoost is also larger under cell splitting (0.048 versus
approximately 0.009 under patient-disjoint evaluation).

These comparisons use the historical cell-weighted metric in both columns;
they must not be compared numerically with the subject-balanced main
leaderboard below. They contrast evaluation protocols, including their
training/test allocation, rather than estimating a causal leakage effect
under a fully matched randomized design. They support a specific conclusion:
held-out-cell performance should not be presented as evidence of new-patient
generalization. They do not show that all model rankings reverse or quantify
optimism for every possible cohort.

\subsection{Reference Performance Under the Patient-Disjoint Contract}
\label{sec:results:subtype_audit}

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares APT-only reference predictions using
subject-balanced pooled Macro-F1 on the same 40 held-out patients.
Coarse/fine scores are 0.298/0.126 for LR and 0.325/0.137 for XGBoost.
Flat, HCE, and Soft-cascade Transformers reach 0.326/0.139,
0.335/0.143, and 0.332/0.145. These point estimates establish reference
performance, not a statistically demonstrated cascade advantage.

Fine prediction remains difficult across references. However, five-lineage

\subsection{Secondary Context: RNA and Patient Summaries}

The paired-input diagnostic is not an extension of the APT-only leaderboard.
Under its capped matched-probe protocol, an MLP reaches coarse/fine
SB-Macro-F1 0.310/0.094 from APT and 0.871/0.584 from RNA; APT+RNA reaches
0.873/0.606. Training-subset-only HVG selection avoids feature-selection
exposure, but RNA also supplies the reference annotations. The comparison
therefore concerns internal label reconstruction, not independent biological
validation or APT algorithm superiority
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:paired_rna_oracle}).

As a secondary patient-level case study, nested subtype summaries reduce
reference-composition error from 0.0231 for a training-mean baseline to
0.0148 (paired reduction 0.0084; 95\% patient-bootstrap CI
$[0.0065,0.0101]$). Their disease Macro-F1 is 0.593 versus 0.477 for
lineage summaries, but the difference is unresolved
($+0.116$; $[-0.015,0.262]$). Strong direct APT disease association is
sensitive to patient-wise centering and cannot be separated from acquisition
effects with the available metadata. Forty patients do not establish clinical
validity. The complete case study and controls remain in
Appendices~\ref{sec:appendix:composition_recovery} and
\ref{sec:appendix:disease_patient_level}, rather than forming a second
headline contribution.
