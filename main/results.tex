\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Patient-Disjoint Hierarchy Prediction Exposes a Granularity Gap}

Coarse lineage recognition is consistently easier than fine subtype recognition
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). For \ourmethod{}, coarse macro-F1 is 0.326 while fine
macro-F1 is 0.152; the matched flat FT-style Transformer reaches 0.323 and
0.147. The corresponding gap appears across model families, indicating that
APT profiles recover broad immune identity more reliably than the finer
transcriptome-derived taxonomy.

Encoder-matched comparisons do not establish an architectural advantage. This
is a benchmark result rather than a failed method claim: after controlling the
backbone, additional hierarchy-specific cascade complexity is not supported on
this cohort.
Relative to the flat Transformer, \ourmethod{} differs by only $+0.0036$ coarse
macro-F1 (paired patient-bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.0078,0.0146]$) and $+0.0055$
fine macro-F1 (95\% CI $[-0.0021,0.0125]$). HCE has the highest coarse point
estimate (0.333), but \ourmethod{}$-$HCE is $-0.0069$
(95\% CI $[-0.0154,0.0026]$) at the coarse level and $+0.0015$
(95\% CI $[-0.0083,0.0047]$) at the fine level. Differences from XGBoost also
do not exclude zero. Exact-path accuracy is 0.248--0.259; HCE has the highest
value, whereas XGBoost has the highest root-excluded hierarchical F1 (0.420)
and lowest subtype-tree distance (2.375). Natural consistency is reported only
as an appendix diagnostic because constrained decoding can alter it without
improving correctness.

\subsection{Why 361{,}792 Cells Still Yield Only 0.15 Subtype Macro-F1}
\label{sec:results:subtype_audit}

Per-subtype F1 is more strongly associated with cell abundance than with
patient coverage in this cohort. Marginal
$\operatorname{Spearman}(F_{1,k},\log n_k)$ versus
$\operatorname{Spearman}(F_{1,k},P_k)$ is 0.486/0.288 for \ourmethod{} and
0.540/0.275 for XGBoost. After rank residualization,
$\rho_{c\mid p}$ is 0.411/0.496 and $\rho_{p\mid c}$ is $-0.051/-0.130$.
Because there are only 27 subtypes and 16 lie at the 40-patient coverage
ceiling, these are exploratory associations rather than causal explanations.

Only 25.0\% of \ourmethod{} errors and 24.6\% of XGBoost errors retain the true
parent. The raw cross-lineage rate depends on the available wrong-class
distribution: against an error-conditioned marginal null, the observed rates
are close to expectation and do not independently demonstrate either hierarchy
preservation or failure. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:subtype_audit} reports
per-subtype patient-clustered intervals, support plots, and the complete error
analysis. Across models, the descriptive coarse-minus-fine macro-F1 gap ranges
from 0.099 to 0.200.

\subsection{Patient Disjointness Changes the Evaluation, Not Just the Split}
\label{sec:results:protocol}

Cell-level splitting inflates coarse/fine macro-F1 by $+0.090/+0.108$ for
\ourmethod{} and $+0.050/+0.069$ for XGBoost
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:protocol_sensitivity}). APT-Bench therefore fixes
patient-disjoint evaluation rather than treating the split as a reporting
detail. Because a patient-disjoint pooled cell metric can still overweight
patients with more cells, an equal-cell sensitivity also samples 1{,}900 cells
per patient over 50 seeds. It changes coarse macro-F1 by at most $+0.005$ and
fine macro-F1 by $-0.007$ to $-0.009$, leaving the qualitative ordering intact.

\subsection{The Label Distribution Confounds Naive Two-Axis Scaling}
\label{sec:results:patient_cell_scaling}

The fair scaling experiment holds outer-test subjects fixed and varies only the
development set. It evaluates fixed-total-cell reallocations, matched doublings,
and a descriptive response surface for LR and XGBoost at both resolutions. Each
point uses 20 matched subject/cell subset seeds; preprocessing and fitting are
repeated, while all cells from untouched test subjects are evaluated. The
design is then frozen for external COMBAT CITE-seq and OneK1K cohorts.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\columnwidth]{figures/class_matched_scaling.pdf}
\caption{Primary class-matched fixed-total scaling control. Each comparison
freezes the observed classes and exact per-class cell quotas before reallocating
the same $T$ training cells from 8 to 32 subjects. Points are changes in
subject-balanced, subject-disjoint OOF macro-F1 at $T=6{,}400$; bars are 95\%
paired subject-bootstrap intervals over fixed outer-test subjects after
averaging 20 LR or 10 XGBoost subset seeds. Ten of 12 empirical seed intervals
include zero; the exceptions are the negative APT fine effects for both models.
The corrected effects are small and model- or cohort-dependent.}
\label{fig:class_matched_scaling}
\end{figure}

As a deliberately naive starting point, the original label-agnostic design
reallocates cells without matching the training label distribution. Moving from 8 to 32 patients improves patient-balanced OOF
macro-F1 in all 12 model--resolution--budget combinations, by 0.006--0.022
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:fair_scaling}). All paired patient-bootstrap and
subset-seed intervals are above zero. Pooled-cell and mean within-patient
sensitivities give the same qualitative direction, with isolated uncertainties
detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:fair_scaling}. These are unmatched
associations to be audited below, not estimates of a pure subject-breadth effect.

The direction transfers to COMBAT despite a different cohort, measurement
technology, and 39-class fine taxonomy. Across 105 donors and 700{,}706 cells,
all 12 RNA effects are positive (0.001--0.018) for LR and XGBoost, and all six
matched ADT LR effects are positive (0.006--0.013). All 18 paired
donor-bootstrap intervals exclude zero. Only 4 of 18 empirical subset-seed
intervals exclude zero, however, showing that donor-bootstrap stability of the
average effect does not eliminate sensitivity to the selected development
subset. COMBAT therefore replicates the fixed-total direction, not a constant
effect size or deterministic advantage for every training subset.

Across 925 healthy OneK1K evaluation donors, seven of eight effects are positive
(0.004--0.009). XGBoost coarse prediction reverses at $T=3{,}200$
($-0.0028$; donor-bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.0036,-0.0017]$), but is positive at
$T=6{,}400$ ($+0.0056$). No OneK1K subset-seed interval excludes zero, exposing
substantial dependence on the selected training donors.

Matched doublings on APT lead to the same descriptive ordering. Averaged across
eligible fixed settings, patient-versus-cell doubling gains are 0.011 versus
0.005 (LR coarse), 0.007 versus 0.003 (LR fine), 0.016 versus 0.008 (XGBoost
coarse), and 0.009 versus 0.006 (XGBoost fine). Fold-adjusted response-surface
coefficients are likewise larger for $\log_2 P$ than $\log_2 C$ in all four
comparisons, although interactions show that neither effect is constant across
the grid. OneK1K matched doublings and response surfaces also favor the donor
axis, but only narrowly for XGBoost coarse prediction. Across APT, COMBAT, and
OneK1K, this naive analysis yields 37 of 38 unmatched fixed-total comparisons
in favor of broader subject coverage. The numerical consistency is misleading
if read as an isolated breadth effect because the sampled subjects and marginal
training label distribution change simultaneously.

The class audit confirms that this distinction matters. At the common
$T=3{,}200$ diagonal, moving from $P=8$ to $P=32$ changes the mean number of
observed fine classes from 26.12 to 26.33 of 27 for APT, 36.97 to 37.34 of 39
for COMBAT, and 27.10 to 27.26 of 30 for OneK1K. At $T=6{,}400$, the
corresponding changes are 26.34 to 26.53, 37.81 to 38.10, and 28.31 to 28.47.
These fractional averages represent fold/seed settings in which a test class
changes from unseen to seen, which can directly affect macro-F1. They measure
only one component of the label-distribution effect: per-class counts and
proportions also change in the unmatched analysis.

We therefore repeat LR after freezing the $P=8$ observed classes and exact
per-class quotas within every fold/seed/task comparison, then repeat the common
$T=6{,}400$ setting with XGBoost as a nonlinear robustness check
(Figure~\ref{fig:class_matched_scaling};
Table~\ref{tab:class_matched_scaling}). Mean donor coverage per observed class
increases substantially while class counts remain identical. At $T=6{,}400$,
the LR/XGBoost effects are $+0.0078/+0.0083$ for APT coarse and
$-0.0050/-0.0058$ for APT fine. COMBAT coarse effects are
$+0.0000/+0.0011$, while fine effects are $-0.0028/-0.0027$.
OneK1K is less stable across models: coarse changes from $+0.0033$ under LR to
$-0.0011$ under XGBoost, and fine changes from $+0.0013$ to $-0.0006$.
Ten of 12 empirical seed intervals at this common total include zero; only the
two APT fine intervals exclude zero, both in the negative direction. Paired
subject-bootstrap intervals for seed-averaged sufficient statistics are shown
in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:class_matched_scaling}. Thus class matching
changes the scientific conclusion: the apparent fine-subtype advantage in the
naive analysis is not an LR artifact, but neither its magnitude nor the coarse
effect supports a universal subject-breadth benefit. Instead, unmatched gains
are partly mediated by changes in label support and class composition, and the
residual class-matched effects remain model- and cohort-dependent. This control
does not attribute the change to unseen classes alone.

\subsection{Nested Cell-Output Transfer Preserves Disease-Associated Variation}
\label{sec:results:composition_bridge}

Composition connects cell typing to patient characterization, but a pooled OOF
pipeline is insufficient: a cell model generating a downstream training
patient's features can have seen the current disease outer-test patients. We
therefore seal each eight-patient outer fold, construct the 32 development
compositions by four-way inner patient-disjoint cross-fitting, and use one cell
model fitted on all 32 development patients only for the sealed test patients.

Under this strict protocol, disease-label-free XGBoost soft subtype composition
reaches patient accuracy/macro-F1/AUROC $0.600/0.593/0.854$, compared with
$0.475/0.477/0.766$ for soft lineage composition. Both exceed their complete
10{,}000-permutation nulls ($p=0.00010$ and $p=0.00020$). Fine granularity is
not established as superior: the paired subtype-minus-lineage macro-F1
difference is $+0.116$ with patient-bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.015,0.262]$. True
subtype composition reaches macro-F1 0.516, so the predicted representation is
not merely a noisier estimate of annotated abundance; it may also retain model
confidence or technical structure.

This is a representation-transfer audit, not evidence of error propagation: it
does not establish a subtype-abundance source, fine-over-coarse utility, or that
better typing improves patient characterization. Hard/soft and predicted/gold
sensitivities are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:composition_bridge}.

Log cell count alone reaches macro-F1 0.531 ($p=0.00010$). A six-feature
nuisance-only model using recovered cell count and patient summaries of the
recorded transcriptomic UMI and detected-gene QC fields reaches macro-F1 0.566
and AUROC 0.859. Predicted subtype composition exceeds the cell-count model by
only $+0.062$ (95\% CI $[-0.087,0.218]$) and the full recorded-QC model by
$+0.027$ (95\% CI $[-0.157,0.215]$). Equal-cell sensitivity, a
global-multinomial composition null, and a fold-ID prediction control do not
establish biological mediation. The defensible finding is therefore narrower:
cell predictions preserve disease-associated variation, but they do not show
incremental value beyond available nuisance summaries.

A stricter conditional audit likewise finds no resolved subtype increment over
recorded QC plus lineage: $\Delta$ macro-F1 $=+0.016$ (95\% paired
patient-bootstrap CI $[-0.120,0.169]$; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:disease_patient_level}).

\paragraph{Identifiability boundary.}
Direct APT discrimination is strong but confounding-sensitive. Patient-wise
centering produces a 35-point drop in majority-vote accuracy, and individual
cells readily identify their source patient. Each participant contributes one
acquisition, while plate, run, date, library, and site covariates and an external
APT cohort are unavailable. Disease, patient, and unrecorded acquisition effects
are therefore not statistically identifiable from this cohort: resampling,
permutation testing, or additional models cannot separate them. We consequently
treat direct APT prediction, compact panels, and attribution only as diagnostics
of a mixed within-cohort signal; full values and controls are confined to
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:disease_patient_level} and
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:minimal_panel}.
