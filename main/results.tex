\subsection{APT-Only Cell Typing in Held-Out Patients}
\label{sec:results:subtype_audit}
\label{sec:results:protocol}

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares the frozen APT-only reference predictions using
subject-balanced pooled Macro-F1. The table addresses model performance on the
same measurement modality and label ontology, not whether APT outperforms RNA.
All evaluations include the same 40 held-out patients across the five folds.
Coarse/fine SB-Macro-F1 is 0.298/0.126 for LR and 0.325/0.137 for
XGBoost. Flat, HCE, and Soft-cascade Transformers reach 0.326/0.139,
0.335/0.143, and 0.332/0.145, respectively. The cascade has the highest fine
point estimate among these rows, while HCE has the highest coarse estimate;
we do not infer a model-specific advantage from these rankings.
Pointwise patient-bootstrap intervals describe uncertainty in each fitted
recipe's performance; they are not evidence that the cascade's individual
components improve prediction.

\subsection{A Separate Paired-Input Modality Reference}

To distinguish input modality from architecture, we compare APT, paired RNA,
and their concatenation with the same two probe families, paired cells,
patient folds, and training budget (Table~\ref{tab:modality_main}). The probe
uses up to 1{,}000 training cells per development patient and selects RNA HVGs
separately inside each inner and outer training subset. Although the metric
matches Table~\ref{tab:main}, preprocessing, training budget, and model recipes
differ; this is not an extension of the APT-only leaderboard.

RNA reconstructs the operational labels more strongly than APT for both
probes. For the MLP, adding APT to RNA improves fine Macro-F1 by $+0.022$
(95\% paired patient-bootstrap CI $[0.013,0.037]$); for the linear SGD probe
the increment is $+0.028$ ($[0.013,0.041]$). The MLP combined input also
exceeds RNA plus within-patient shuffled APT by $+0.010$ ($[0.002,0.017]$),
whereas this contrast is unresolved for SGD. Full noise and pairing controls
are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:paired_rna_oracle}.

These results support complementary association under a matched probe, not
APT superiority over RNA or an information-theoretic limit of the assay.
Training-fold feature selection addresses preprocessing exposure, but RNA
also supplies the reference annotations and therefore cannot independently
validate their biological accuracy. Chance-adjusted resolution diagnostics
are retained in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:granularity_chance}.

\subsection{Secondary Application: Patient-Level Summaries}
\label{sec:results:composition_bridge}

We first evaluate patient composition without using disease labels.
Nested soft subtype summaries have equal-patient, equal-subtype mean absolute
error 0.0148, versus 0.0231 for the outer-training patients' mean reference
composition. The paired error reduction is 0.0084 (95\% patient-bootstrap CI
$[0.0065,0.0101]$). This establishes improvement over a training-only constant
reference for composition reconstruction, not a disease-prediction benefit.
The lineage reduction is unresolved. Definitions and coverage diagnostics
are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:composition_recovery}.

As an exploratory disease readout, nested predicted subtype composition
reaches patient Macro-F1 0.593 versus 0.477 for predicted lineage composition.
Their paired difference is unresolved ($+0.116$; $[-0.015,0.262]$), and
conditioning both representations on recorded QC reduces the increment to
$+0.016$ ($[-0.120,0.169]$). Table~\ref{tab:disease} reports reference
compositions and nuisance baselines. Thus cell-model outputs carry
disease-associated information, but do not establish incremental fine-subtype
utility or a relationship between improved typing and improved diagnosis.

Direct APT summaries show stronger within-cohort association: patient-median
APT plus LR reaches accuracy 0.950. However, centering reduces a separate
cell-level LR majority-vote readout from 40/40 to 26/40 correct patients,
and an identity diagnostic reaches 88.4\% on held-out cells from known
patients. These controls implicate patient-specific expression structure
without identifying its biological or technical origin. The independent
disease sample remains 40; acquisition metadata and external APT validation
are outstanding. Full controls and secondary budget diagnostics are in
Appendices~\ref{sec:appendix:disease_patient_level} and
\ref{sec:appendix:minimal_panel}.

\subsection{Supporting Evaluation Audits}

Cell-random splitting inflates historical cell-weighted coarse/fine Macro-F1
by $+0.090/+0.108$ for the soft cascade and $+0.050/+0.069$ for XGBoost.
This motivates patient-disjoint evaluation independently of the choice to
balance patients at scoring time. Equal-cell subsampling, error-conditioned
hierarchy nulls, and class-level diagnostics remain available in the appendix.

Sampling studies on APT, COMBAT, and OneK1K examine how subject breadth, cell
depth, and training-label allocation change performance. Natural fixed-total
and class-quota-matched policies estimate different allocation contrasts;
the latter also changes within-class donor allocation. Full grids, uncertainty
definitions, extended frontiers, and simulations are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:scaling_support}. They qualify resource-allocation
claims and do not constitute external validation of APT disease prediction.
