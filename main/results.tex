\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Patient-Disjoint Typing Exposes a Granularity Gap}

Coarse lineage recognition is consistently easier than fine subtype recognition
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). The soft-cascade recipe reaches coarse/fine macro-F1
0.326/0.152, compared with 0.323/0.147 for the flat Transformer. A paired-RNA
linear probe localizes this gap: under the same folds and cells, APT reaches
0.288/0.104 and RNA reaches 0.790/0.442. Combining APT with RNA further raises
fine macro-F1 to 0.473 (RNA-only difference $+0.031$, 95\% CI
$[0.016,0.046]$). Thus APT has complementary fine-resolution information, but
the label-generating RNA modality reconstructs the taxonomy much better and
still retains a large coarse--fine gap. This is an internal reconstructability
diagnostic, not independent validation of transcriptome-derived labels
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:paired_rna_oracle}).

Backbone- and budget-matched comparisons do not establish superiority for the
complete soft-cascade recipe. Its coarse/fine differences from the flat model
are $+0.0036/+0.0055$, with paired patient-bootstrap intervals spanning zero;
comparisons with HCE and XGBoost are likewise unresolved. Because only the
soft-cascade recipe includes cross-disease contrastive regularization, these are
recipe comparisons rather than component ablations. Exact-path accuracy is
0.248--0.259, and XGBoost has the lowest subtype-tree distance.

\subsection{Subtype Difficulty Is Not Explained by Cell Count Alone}
\label{sec:results:subtype_audit}

Per-subtype F1 is more strongly associated with cell abundance than patient
coverage, but 16 of 27 subtypes occur in all 40 patients, limiting the latter's
variation. After rank residualization, cell-support associations remain positive
(0.411 for the soft cascade; 0.496 for XGBoost), whereas patient-coverage
associations are near zero. Only about one quarter of errors retain the true
parent; however, the observed cross-lineage rates are close to an
error-conditioned marginal null and do not independently demonstrate hierarchy
preservation or failure. These exploratory support and error analyses, including
clustered intervals and full per-subtype results, are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:subtype_audit}.

\subsection{Patient Disjointness Changes the Evaluation}
\label{sec:results:protocol}

Cell-random splitting inflates coarse/fine macro-F1 by $+0.090/+0.108$ for the
soft cascade and $+0.050/+0.069$ for XGBoost. Equal-cell-per-patient evaluation
changes patient-disjoint macro-F1 by at most 0.009, so the conclusion is not
driven by a few high-cell-count patients
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:protocol_sensitivity}).

\subsection{Label Distribution Confounds Naive Two-Axis Scaling}
\label{sec:results:patient_cell_scaling}

We first reallocate a fixed number of training cells from 8 to 32 subjects
without matching the class distribution. Across APT, COMBAT, and OneK1K, 37 of
38 model--resolution--budget comparisons favor greater subject breadth. Yet
these comparisons simultaneously change observed labels, per-class counts, and
class proportions. Training-subset intervals are also substantially less stable
than test-subject bootstrap intervals. The 37/38 direction is therefore an audit
target, not a pure subject-breadth effect.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\columnwidth]{figures/class_matched_scaling.pdf}
\caption{Class-matched fixed-total scaling. Each paired comparison freezes the
observed classes and exact per-class quotas before reallocating $T=6{,}400$
training cells from 8 to 32 subjects. Points are changes in subject-balanced
pooled OOF macro-F1; 95\% joint intervals resample both the training-subset seed
and paired held-out subjects. All 12 intervals include zero.}
\label{fig:class_matched_scaling}
\end{figure}

Class matching changes the conclusion (Figure~\ref{fig:class_matched_scaling}).
At $T=6{,}400$, LR/XGBoost effects are $+0.0078/+0.0083$ for APT coarse but
$-0.0050/-0.0058$ for APT fine. COMBAT effects are approximately zero for
coarse and $-0.0028/-0.0027$ for fine; OneK1K effects are small and change sign
across models. All 12 joint intervals include zero. Thus negative APT and COMBAT
fine point estimates are not LR-specific, but no corrected effect is resolved
after propagating both training-subset and held-out-subject uncertainty. The
unmatched gain is partly mediated by the broader label-distribution change, not
only whether a rare class is observed.

The donor-rich cohorts do not reveal a delayed LR benefit. Extending the same
class-matched design to 64 COMBAT and 128 OneK1K training donors yields adjacent
effects between $-0.00065$ and $+0.00123$, with every joint interval spanning
zero. This supports a tested-range plateau under fixed totals and label quotas,
not a universal optimal number of subjects. Complete grids, matched doublings,
class-support audits, metric sensitivities, and uncertainty decompositions are
reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:fair_scaling}--
\ref{sec:appendix:extended_subject_frontier}.

\subsection{Nested Cell Outputs Retain Disease-Associated Variation}
\label{sec:results:composition_bridge}

We connect cell typing to patient characterization using strict nested
cross-fitting: each eight-patient outer fold remains sealed while compositions
for the 32 development patients are generated by inner patient-disjoint models.
Disease-label-free XGBoost subtype probabilities reach patient macro-F1 0.593,
versus 0.477 for lineage probabilities, but the subtype-minus-lineage difference
is unresolved ($+0.116$, 95\% CI $[-0.015,0.262]$). Gold subtype composition
reaches 0.516, indicating that predicted probabilities may also carry confidence
or technical structure rather than serving only as abundance estimates.

Log cell count and recorded RNA-QC summaries reach macro-F1 0.531 and 0.566.
Subtype composition exceeds them by only $+0.062$ and $+0.027$, respectively,
with both intervals spanning zero; a conditional QC-plus-lineage comparison is
also unresolved. The defensible result is representation transfer, not subtype
mediation, fine-over-coarse utility, or error propagation. The full table and
controls are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:composition_bridge}.

Direct APT discrimination is stronger but confounding-sensitive: patient-wise
centering reduces majority-vote accuracy by 35 points, and cells readily predict
their source patient. Because disease, patient, and unrecorded acquisition are
not identifiable in this single-acquisition cohort, direct prediction, compact
panels, and attribution remain diagnostics of a mixed within-cohort signal
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:disease_patient_level} and
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:minimal_panel}).
