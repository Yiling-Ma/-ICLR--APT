\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Recovering Immune-Cell Identity from APT}
\label{sec:results:subtype_audit}
\label{sec:results:protocol}

The first question is how well the aptamer measurements reconstruct the
reference cell identities in new patients. Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the
fixed five-fold evaluation. Coarse/fine macro-F1 is 0.299/0.132 for LR and
0.322/0.143 for XGBoost. The soft-cascade recipe reaches 0.326/0.152,
compared with 0.323/0.147 for the flat Transformer. The designated comparisons
with HCE and XGBoost do not reach Holm-adjusted significance; the Flat
comparison is descriptive. These results support evaluation of cell identity
from APT, not a model-specific algorithmic advantage.

All evaluated models have lower raw fine than coarse F1. However, the five-
and 27-class tasks differ in class count, imbalance, and label boundaries.
Their raw difference does not isolate biological resolution or show that the
assay intrinsically lacks subtype information. Subtype support, root-excluded
hierarchical F1, and error-conditioned hierarchy diagnostics are reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:subtype_audit}.

\subsection{What the Paired Transcriptome Adds}

Under the same patient-disjoint linear-probe diagnostic, paired RNA achieves
subject-balanced pooled coarse/fine macro-F1 of 0.790/0.442, compared with
0.288/0.104 for APT. Combining the two inputs reaches 0.794/0.473; the fine
increment over RNA is $+0.031$ (95\% paired patient-bootstrap CI
$[0.016,0.046]$). These are matched-probe results, not scores directly comparable
with the cell-weighted full-training models in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

The input-dependent reconstruction difference is informative, but its scope
is internal. The reference labels were constructed from RNA, and the fixed
HVG representation was selected during data development. RNA is consequently
not independent ground truth validation or a leakage-free benchmark baseline.
The combined-input result motivates further study of complementary measurements,
without demonstrating an intrinsic information limit or clinical benefit.

A marginal-chance adjustment reduces the APT coarse-minus-fine difference
from 0.184 to 0.051 (95\% interval $[0.030,0.080]$). Even that residual does
not match task cardinality or within-lineage difficulty. Complete input and
resolution diagnostics appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:paired_rna_oracle} and
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:granularity_chance}.

\subsection{Connecting Cell Profiles to Patient Characterization}
\label{sec:results:composition_bridge}

We next ask whether the cell-model outputs retain information associated
with the patient's disease group. Strict nested cross-fitting constructs
training and test patient representations without exposing disease-test
patients to the cell-typing model. Table~\ref{tab:disease} compares these
representations with transcriptomic reference compositions and recorded
nuisance controls, using one prediction per patient.

\input{tables/disease}

Predicted soft subtype composition reaches patient macro-F1 0.593, compared
with 0.477 for predicted lineage composition and 0.516 for reference subtype
composition. The subtype-minus-lineage difference is $+0.116$ with a 95\%
paired patient-bootstrap CI of $[-0.015,0.262]$. Recovered cell count alone
reaches 0.531 and recorded QC summaries reach 0.566. After adding recorded QC
to both representations, the subtype-minus-lineage increment is only $+0.016$
(CI $[-0.120,0.169]$).

The bridge therefore identifies disease-associated information in cell-model
outputs, but does not demonstrate an incremental benefit from fine subtypes.
Soft predictions can encode confidence patterns or technical structure as
well as cell abundance. Predicted composition exceeding reference composition
in point estimate is not proof of superior biological characterization, and
these comparisons do not establish that reducing cell-typing error improves
disease prediction. The bridge is representation transfer, not a validated
error-propagation relationship.

\subsection{Direct Disease Association and Its Alternative Explanations}

Direct APT representations distinguish disease groups much more strongly than
the composition summaries: patient-median APT plus LR reaches accuracy 0.950
and macro-F1 0.948. A cell-level LR disease-source classifier yields correct
patient majority votes for 40/40 held-out patients. These are within-cohort
associations, not independently validated diagnostic sensitivity or specificity.

Two controls change their interpretation. Subtracting each patient's own
per-aptamer median reduces LR patient majority-vote accuracy from 40/40 to
26/40, showing dependence on absolute-expression structure. In a separate
within-patient cell-split diagnostic, patient identity can be predicted with
88.4\% accuracy. This identity result does not itself prove leakage in the
patient-disjoint disease experiment; it shows that cells carry strong
patient-specific signatures.

Patient-disjoint folds prevent the same person's cells appearing in training
and test, but cannot disentangle disease-correlated acquisition effects.
The metadata lack plate, run, date, library, operator, and site fields, and
centering can remove biological as well as technical variation. The strongest
disease-associated signal is therefore not attributable to either source.
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:disease_patient_level} provides the complete controls.
Feature and inference-cell budget diagnostics remain in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:minimal_panel}; compression of this mixed signal is
not evidence of a diagnostic biomarker panel.

\subsection{Reliability Across Cells and Subjects}

Cell-random splitting inflates coarse/fine macro-F1 by $+0.090/+0.108$ for
the soft cascade and $+0.050/+0.069$ for XGBoost. By comparison,
equal-cell-per-patient evaluation changes patient-disjoint scores by at most
0.009. Splitting at the patient level is thus essential for the cell-to-patient
evaluation, not interchangeable with reweighting the same cells.

Supporting experiments on APT, COMBAT, and OneK1K examine a separate resource
question: allocating a fixed cell budget across more subjects. Natural
allocation and a class-quota-matched policy yield different breadth estimates;
the latter also changes within-class donor allocation. Joint resampling ranges
describe subset and test-subject stability, not confidence intervals for the
mean effect. These findings caution against treating more cells as more
independent patients or interpreting a scaling curve without its label
distribution. Full grids, extended donor frontiers, and simulations are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:scaling_support}; they support evaluation design
rather than external clinical validation.
