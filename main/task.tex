\subsection{Blood Profiles and Reference Labels}
\label{sec:data:description}

APT-Bench is derived from peripheral-blood specimens from 40 participants in
five cancer groups (CRC, GC, HCC, PC, and BTC) and healthy controls, with 6--8
participants per group. The paired assay measures transcriptomic state and 293
target-specific nucleic-acid aptamers on the same cells. Transcriptomic counts
were processed with Seurat \citep{hao2021integrated}; predicted doublets and
low-quality cells were removed, and clusters were manually annotated using
canonical markers from prior PBMC and pan-cancer studies
\citep{mcginnis2019doubletfinder,zhang2024pan,yang2024pan}. These annotations
define five lineages and \numsubtypes{} subtypes. They are operational reference
labels, not independently measured biological truth.

The linked post-QC cohort contains 374{,}854 cells. Excluding the uncertain
\emph{Unknown} subtype leaves 361{,}792 benchmark cells. Each subtype has one
fixed parent lineage. Disease is a six-class patient attribute, not the root of
the cell hierarchy: any given lineage can occur in multiple disease groups.
The benchmark therefore connects cell and patient tasks through aggregation,
rather than imposing a disease-to-lineage-to-subtype label tree.

The construction checklist and unresolved acquisition and annotation fields
are recorded in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:dataset_documentation}. In particular,
the analyzed data do not establish a validated one-drop input-volume
requirement. The scope here is characterization from the profiled blood cells,
not end-to-end validation of a small-volume diagnostic assay.

\subsection{From Cell Identity to Patient State}
\label{sec:data:contract}
\label{sec:data:protocols}

The evaluation has two connected levels. \textbf{Cell identity}
predicts lineage and subtype from APT alone. \textbf{Patient characterization}
predicts disease-group membership from patient summaries of measurements or
cell-model outputs. The latter is an exploratory association task: with one
acquisition per participant and incomplete acquisition metadata, disease
biology and technical structure cannot be identified separately.
Open-set subtype discovery is outside the contract; all primary cell tasks
use the fixed five-lineage/27-subtype ontology.

Five frozen disease-stratified folds each hold out eight patients. All cells
from a patient remain together; preprocessing, model selection, and fitting
are restricted to development patients. We pool predictions across the five
out-of-fold (OOF) partitions. A cell-random split is retained only as a leakage
diagnostic, not as evidence of generalization to new patients. The release
interface records cell and patient IDs, ontology, folds, predictions, and
metrics (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:benchmark_release}). Full data access and a
portable end-to-end evaluator remain release requirements.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Units}
\label{sec:data:imbalance}

Coarse and fine macro-F1 are the primary cell metrics. Exact-path accuracy and
subtype-tree distance describe joint hierarchy errors; root-excluded
hierarchical F1 and consistency are supplementary. The main hierarchy table
uses cell-weighted pooled OOF predictions and 2{,}000 paired patient bootstraps.
The designated comparison family contrasts the soft-cascade recipe with HCE
and XGBoost at both resolutions, with Holm correction across four tests.
Flat and other classical comparisons are descriptive only.

Patient characterization uses one prediction per patient, with patient-level
accuracy, macro-F1, and macro-AUROC. Its effective disease sample is 40, not
361{,}792. Nested model selection prevents direct test exposure but does not
remove confounding or uncertainty from exploratory analysis choices.

Subject-balanced sensitivities first normalize each held-out subject's
confusion matrix to unit mass before pooling. This differs from cell-weighted
pooling and from averaging within-subject macro-F1. The paired-RNA probe and
supporting scaling analyses use this subject-balanced pooled metric; their
scores are not directly interchangeable with the main cell-weighted table.
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:protocol_sensitivity} gives the definitions.

\subsection{Supporting Reliability Analyses}

The paired-RNA analysis is an internal reconstruction diagnostic, since RNA
also supplies the reference annotations. Its data-development HVG selection
means it is not an independent, leakage-free RNA benchmark baseline.
Centering, patient-identity prediction, and recorded QC controls probe
alternative explanations for disease association. Feature-panel and
inference-cell subsampling are secondary redundancy diagnostics, not evidence
of reduced training requirements or clinically efficient assays.

Subject--cell sampling controls are reported separately in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:scaling_support}. They compare natural fixed-total
allocation with class-quota-matched allocation and distinguish stability
across training subsets from confidence intervals for mean effects.
COMBAT CITE-seq and OneK1K provide external sampling evaluations
\citep{combat2022blood,yazar2022onek1k}, not external disease validation of the
APT assay. Their RNA experiments use restricted 293-gene representations.
