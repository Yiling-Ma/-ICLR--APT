\section{Problem Setup}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Dataset and Prediction Targets}
\label{sec:data:description}

\paragraph{Cohort construction and specimen source.}
APT-Bench is derived from peripheral-blood clinical specimens collected from 40
participants: patients with colorectal cancer (CRC), gastric cancer (GC),
hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), pancreatic cancer (PC), or biliary tract cancer
(BTC), and healthy controls (Normal). The six groups contain 6--8 participants
each. Clinical specimens were processed into single-cell suspensions before
assay preparation. The study workflow documents quality control using the
forward/side-scatter cell profile and viable-cell proportion before library
construction.

\paragraph{Paired single-cell assay and label construction.}
The assay jointly measures transcriptomic state and binding of 293
target-specific nucleic-acid aptamers on the same cells. Transcriptome and
aptamer-barcode libraries are constructed separately and linked at the cell
level by their shared barcodes. Transcriptomic counts were processed and
clustered with the standard Seurat workflow \citep{hao2021integrated}; predicted
doublets were removed with DoubletFinder \citep{mcginnis2019doubletfinder}, and
low-quality cells were filtered using per-cell UMI count, detected-gene count,
and mitochondrial-transcript fraction. Investigators then manually annotated
clusters from canonical subtype marker expression reported in prior PBMC and
pan-cancer studies \citep{zhang2024pan,yang2024pan}. These transcriptome-derived
annotations define the lineage and subtype targets, while disease labels come
from participant-level cohort assignment. The benchmark input contains only
the 293 APT measurements; transcriptomic measurements construct the reference
labels but are never exposed to a prediction model. Exact parameters and label
limitations are documented in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:dataset_documentation}.

\paragraph{Benchmark cohort.}
The linked post-QC cohort contains 374{,}854 cells. After excluding the
transcriptome-derived \emph{Unknown} subtype label, which denotes uncertain
annotation rather than a validated biological state, the main benchmark contains
361{,}792 cells. Each cell is represented by a 293-dimensional APT expression
vector. Expression values are log-transformed and standardized feature-wise
using statistics estimated from the training partition only. The 293 aptamers
are designed probes rather than an arbitrary learned basis.

APT-Bench contains two kinds of targets at different observational units.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient-level disease-association audit.} Each patient is assigned to one of six disease groups. Because every participant contributes a single acquisition and batch metadata are unavailable, this target supports within-cohort association and confounding analyses, not biological attribution or clinical validation.
    \item \textbf{Cell-level hierarchy prediction.} Each cell is assigned to one of five coarse immune lineages and one of \numsubtypes{} fine subtypes. Every subtype maps to exactly one parent lineage through a manually curated lookup table. The same subtype names and operational definitions are used across all six disease groups rather than being redefined by disease.
\end{itemize}
Disease is therefore not treated as the root of a three-level hierarchy. It is a separate patient-level label observed on the same blood draw.

\subsection{Benchmark Contract and Extensibility}
\label{sec:data:contract}

APT-Bench fixes the unit of input, prediction target, split, and metric for each
track: five-lineage and 27-subtype prediction from one APT vector per cell;
two-axis scaling over training patients and cells per patient; and strict
cross-fitted aggregation from cell predictions to six-class patient-level
characterization with gold-composition and technical controls. Open-set subtype
discovery is outside the current contract; all primary hierarchy tasks use the
fixed 5-lineage/27-subtype ontology. Outer-test-isolated aptamer measurement and
inference-cell budgets are secondary sensitivities. The machine-readable manifest at
\texttt{benchmark/splits/patient\_folds.json} assigns every pseudonymous patient
to one outer fold; all selection remains inside development patients. Portable
components include patient-clustered uncertainty, hierarchy diagnostics,
pseudoreplication and fingerprint audits, and outer-test-isolated budget curves. The full
interface and release contract appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:benchmark_release}.
This cohort cannot estimate cross-site or cross-platform generalization.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocols}
\label{sec:data:protocols}

We evaluate models under two main protocols with different purposes.

\paragraph{Patient-level 5-fold cross-validation.}
This is the primary protocol for all headline claims. Patients are partitioned into five disease-stratified folds, each containing eight held-out test patients. At every fold, preprocessing statistics, model fitting, and hyperparameter selection use only the training and validation patients from the remaining folds. We pool out-of-fold predictions across all five test folds, yielding a patient-disjoint evaluation spanning all 40 patients.

\paragraph{Cell-level split control.}
As a protocol-sensitivity control, cells are randomly split irrespective of patient identity. Cells from the same patient can therefore appear in both training and evaluation sets. We do not treat this as a realistic deployment protocol. Its only role is to quantify performance inflation caused by leakage.

\paragraph{Training patient--cell scaling.}
The original $4\times4$ surface is retained as a descriptive sensitivity, but
its unequal endpoints do not compare patient and cell effects. The primary fair
design uses $P\in\{8,16,32\}$ outer-development patients and
$C\in\{100,200,400,800,1600\}$ uniformly sampled cells per patient. Its
fixed-total diagonals hold $T=PC\in\{3200,6400,12800\}$ exactly constant, and
the full grid also supports matched doublings of one axis while holding the
other fixed. Within every outer fold, 20 disease-stratified patient orderings
and uniform within-patient cell permutations are nested across budgets.
Preprocessing is fit only on sampled training cells; frozen Logistic Regression
and XGBoost configurations are refit for coarse and fine prediction; and every
cell from the same eight untouched outer-test patients is evaluated. We report
pooled cell-weighted, patient-balanced pooled, and mean within-patient macro-F1.
Empirical subset-seed intervals and paired patient-clustered
bootstrap intervals answer different uncertainty questions and are reported
separately. This training-time design is distinct from inference subsampling in
the compact-panel analysis.

The fixed-total grid does not by itself isolate subject breadth: adding subjects
can add rare labels and alter class proportions. We therefore run a separate
class-matched control at the two cross-cohort totals,
$T\in\{3200,6400\}$. For each outer-fold, seed, task, and $T$, the nested
$P=8$ development subset freezes the observed class set and an integer
per-class quota $T_k$. The identical $T_k$ values are then sampled without
replacement at $P=8,16,32$, using capacity-constrained round-robin allocation
to spread each class over as many selected subjects as possible. This makes
class support, counts, and proportions identical within each paired comparison;
only the subjects contributing those cells change. We refit class-weighted
multinomial Logistic Regression and report subject-balanced OOF macro-F1,
empirical intervals over 20 subset seeds, and paired subject-bootstrap
intervals. Logistic Regression is used as a focused sampling control rather
than as another model-comparison family.

To test portability beyond a single assay and cohort, we apply the same grid to
the public COMBAT CITE-seq blood atlas \citep{combat2022blood}. After a frozen
quality and coverage audit, 105 donors and 700{,}706 cells are assigned to five
donor-disjoint folds. The primary external replication uses 293 RNA features
selected only on 10 separate calibration donors; a matched-modality sensitivity
uses all 192 released antibody-derived tag (ADT) features on exactly the same
cells, donors, labels, and folds. Full construction and exclusion rules are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:combat_scaling}.

A second external replication uses 925 evaluation donors and 1{,}199{,}468
cells from the healthy OneK1K PBMC atlas \citep{yazar2022onek1k}. It uses
pool- and donor-disjoint folds, calibration-only selection of 293 RNA features,
and the same LR/XGBoost protocol. A coverage audit limits $C$ to 800 and the
common fixed totals to 3{,}200 and 6{,}400; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:combat_scaling}
gives the construction and released-label limitations.

\subsection{Class Distribution}
\label{sec:data:imbalance}

Because the subtype distribution is long-tailed, we use macro-F1 as the primary metric for coarse-lineage and fine-subtype prediction. Accuracy is secondary. Primary point estimates pool cells and are therefore cell-weighted despite patient-disjoint splits; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:protocol_sensitivity} reports equal-cell-per-patient sensitivity results. For uncertainty, we compute 95\% confidence intervals from 2{,}000 paired patient-level bootstrap resamples over the 40 pooled test patients. The prespecified architecture-audit family asks whether the most structured neural reference, \ourmethod{}, outperforms HCE or XGBoost at the coarse and fine endpoints. Two-sided bootstrap $p$-values use twice the smaller tail probability with a plus-one correction and are Holm-adjusted across these four tests; comparisons outside this family are exploratory. This family audits an architecture claim rather than presuming one, and all four audited advantages fail to exclude zero.

From the same pooled predictions, joint hierarchy metrics are exact-path
accuracy, root-excluded example-averaged lineage/subtype F1, and subtype-tree distance;
support correlations, sibling errors, and the coarse--fine gap are exploratory
difficulty diagnostics.

We audit disease association at the patient level because disease labels belong
to patients rather than cells. Cell-level source-prediction accuracies are
included only as supplementary confounding diagnostics and are not interpreted
as though hundreds of thousands of cells were independent disease observations.
