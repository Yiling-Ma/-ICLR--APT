\section{Completed Sequential Controls on Three Cohorts}
\label{sec:sequential_completed}

\paragraph{Completion and audit.}
The frozen sequential experiment is complete for APT, COMBAT RNA, and
OneK1K: 2,400 fits per cohort and 7,200 in total. Each cohort crosses five
patient-disjoint folds, ten paired subset seeds, two resolutions, three
models (LR, XGBoost, MLP), two subject budgets ($P=8,32$), and four sampling
policies at a fixed total of $T=6{,}400$ training cells. Independent metadata
checks passed for completeness, fixed configurations, class quotas, paired
training-manifest hashes across models, and summary consistency. All audited
runs realized their nominal subject count. These checks do not independently
revalidate training execution or every prediction; the frozen runner checks
train/test subject separation and the aggregator checks OOF pairing and
confusion matrices. Source hashes and audit scope accompany the summaries.

\paragraph{Four distinct allocation policies.}
A samples uniformly from pooled cells in the selected subject set. B fixes
the class support observed in the eight-subject development reference, with
a one-cell-per-class floor, but leaves remaining counts unconstrained. C
fixes exact integer class quotas derived from that same development
reference and samples uniformly within each class. D retains C's quotas
but allocates cells by capacity-constrained round-robin sampling across
randomly ordered donors within each class. Thus C and D have identical
class counts and proportions but different donor allocation. Shared cell
priorities and nested subject sets pair the comparisons. A is a pooled-cell
policy, not the equal-cells-per-subject policy used in earlier unmatched
tables; the historical results are not interchangeable with this experiment.
Standardization is fitted on training cells only. The fixed external RNA
representations and limited model budgets remain constraints on interpretation.

\paragraph{Joint intervals for mean effects.}
For each seed, subject confusion matrices are normalized to unit mass and
pooled before computing Macro-F1 and the paired $32-8$ difference. The point
estimate averages ten such differences. Each of 2,000 bootstrap replicates
resamples ten seeds and held-out subjects with replacement, using shared
multiplicities across budgets and policies. These are intervals for the
mean over the fitted seed collection, conditional on the cohort and fixed
folds, not single-seed stability ranges or uncertainty from retraining on
new cohorts. They do not account fully for dependence induced by overlapping
training folds. Intervals are pointwise, without multiplicity correction;
bootstrap positive fractions are neither posterior probabilities nor
calibrated hypothesis-test $p$-values.

\paragraph{Exact quotas do not alone remove the fine-resolution gains.}
All nine fine-resolution C estimates are positive, and their pointwise
intervals exclude zero. For APT, the LR/XGBoost/MLP C effects are
$+0.0093/+0.0065/+0.0087$, whereas D gives
$-0.0048/-0.0044/-0.0031$. Across APT and COMBAT, all six fine D point
estimates are negative, but only three intervals exclude zero. All three
OneK1K fine D estimates remain positive with intervals spanning zero.
The fine $\Delta_C-\Delta_D$ contrasts are positive in all nine cases;
eight pointwise intervals exclude zero. Consequently, the reversal cannot
be attributed solely to matching class support or proportions: within-class
donor allocation materially changes the comparison. These completed results
qualify interpretations based only on historical unmatched and fully matched
endpoints. They do not establish that patient breadth is universally
beneficial or unhelpful.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The sequential differences describe an ordered set of sampling-policy
changes, not separately identified biological mechanisms or causal mediation.
Their magnitudes need not be invariant to ordering, quota reference, or
training budget. No equivalence or saturation claim follows from an interval
containing zero, and no attenuation ratio is reported when its denominator
may be near zero. The main APT-Bench paper remains centered on cell typing;
these completed allocation experiments are documented only in this report.
